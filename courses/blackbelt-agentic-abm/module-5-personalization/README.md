# Offer & Content Personalization

*Module 05 · Offer & Content Personalization*

Account research and KPIs are only useful once they change what you actually say to an account. This module covers three types of offer — industry, persona, and company + individual — and the content that supports each one, from the first lead magnet to the proposal that closes the deal.

## Where this fits in the six ABM processes

Module 2 built the target account list. Module 3 turned that list into account intelligence. Module 4 set goals and KPIs. This module is process 4 of 6: turn everything you now know about an account into an offer and content that actually speaks to it — instead of a generic pitch with a mail-merge tag. Module 6 then assembles this content into a sequenced program.

## Case in point: what full personalization looks like end to end

The source training uses a published Baker Hughes ABM case as its opening example, for an industrial additives business unit. The campaign segmented content by industry (automotive, aviation, oil & gas, electronics), built personas around each buyer's underlying psychographic driver — mainly fear of non-compliance and a need to de-risk their own job — and synced messaging to funnel stage (a white-paper case study at awareness, an infographic at interest, a compliance-focused white paper at consideration). Reported results: a Content Marketing Awards finalist, 600+ content downloads, 500+ new contacts, 100+ MQLs, 11 deals in pipeline, 3 signed contracts, and 1,300%+ ROI. The lesson the module draws from it: the right content, for the right persona, at the right time, beats volume every time.

## Customer personas

A persona is a portrait of what a decision-maker thinks about, does, wants and fears — written in their own vocabulary, not yours. The stats the module leads with: roughly 80% of successful B2B companies document their personas; content personalized to a persona is around 6x more effective in outbound; and persona-personalized email sees roughly 5x higher open rates.

### Where persona information actually comes from

- 5-10 individual interviews with real customers and lost deals — ideally run by someone outside your own team, so people are candid
- Internal systems: CRM notes, win/loss analysis, what documents get requested and in what order
- Sitting in on sales calls, or listening to call recordings
- A "day in the life" style write-up of the role
- Job postings for the role, and the person's own social profiles and communities
- What your international peers and competitors already know about the same role

### Three formats for writing a persona down

The module teaches three interchangeable templates — pick one and use it consistently:

- **Variant 1 — 5 rings of buying insight** (Adele Revella, *[Buyer Personas](https://www.amazon.com/Buyer-Personas-Revised-Expanded-Customers/dp/1394236336)* — see [further reading](../module-7-further-reading/README.md)): priority initiatives, success factors, perceived barriers, buyer's journey, and decision criteria. Revella's own book uses Boon Edam's published "Architect" persona case as its worked example — real quotes from real architects about choosing revolving-door manufacturers.
- **Variant 2 — Pains / Objections / Important-to-know**: the format this module's homework uses. For each persona: at least 3 current pains or problems connected to your product, 3 objections that come up in negotiation, and 3 things the decision-maker absolutely needs to know about you — plus which other products and companies you get compared to.
- **Variant 3 — empathy map canvas**: what the persona thinks & feels, hears, sees, and says & does, plus their pains and gains.

### Insight aggregator: from raw quotes to a usable insight

1. Transcribe every interview (tools like Sembly or Fireflies handle this) and tag each quote by what type of insight it reveals.
2. Pull out the quotes where someone got specific or showed real emotion; drop them into an "insight aggregator" sheet.
3. Write a short "headline insight" describing what matters in each quote.
4. Group similar headline insights together — in practice, 4-5 quotes from different people usually support the same underlying insight.
5. Rewrite the grouped insight as one precise, detailed statement of what this persona actually expects.

### Drafting a persona with AI: a reusable prompt

The source material teaches a RISE-style prompt (Role, Instruction, Steps, Expectation) for a first-draft persona profile. Give the model your business context first, then:

- **Role** — "You are a strategic analyst researching a target customer for a B2B company."
- **Instruction** — "{target persona} is a typical decision-maker at companies in {industry} that we sell to. Build a detailed profile focused on the pains, objections, and must-know facts needed for personalized outreach."
- **Steps** — gather (reports, interviews, public statements) → analyze (priorities, motivations, objections) → synthesize into Pains / Objections / Important-to-know blocks.
- **Expectation** — a 50-60 point summary across the three blocks, sourced from material no older than two years.

Treat the output as a first draft only — verify it against your own interviews before you build messaging on top of it. Homework 5 below has you run this exact workflow through MegaBrain.

## The personalization hierarchy

Industry-level trends and events create company-level initiatives; company initiatives change what a specific persona is measured on day to day. Your offer should personalize at the same three layers, from broad and qualitative at the top to narrow and quantified at the bottom:

| Offer type | What it contains | Dominant funnel stage |
|---|---|---|
| Industry offer | Broad, quality-over-numbers value; not built to sell — built to earn the next click (visit the site, take a call, join a webinar, download an e-book) | Awareness / Interest |
| Persona offer | Built around one functional role's perception of value; requires real understanding of that persona's fears, motivations, expectations and priorities | Interest / Consideration |
| Company + individual offer | Solves one named person's problem at one named company; built entirely from account-intelligence data; designed to break a specific mental model, and must be expressed in numbers — revenue, cost, risk | Decision |

### Content by customer-journey stage

| Stage | Our goal | Role of content | Example content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Attract the audience | Spark interest; be a trustworthy source that understands the market | Articles, blog posts, checklists, infographics, podcasts, thought leadership, guides, research |
| Interest | Get the lead | Show a concrete solution to the persona's needs and pains | Explainer videos, assessment tools, product pages, frameworks, workbooks, virtual/in-person events |
| Consideration | Build brand preference | Show the value we can deliver and what makes us different | Solution guides, ROI examples, webinars, case studies, testimonials, technical data, comparisons |
| Decision | Convert to buyer | Show value in money and help our internal champion get approval | ROI calculators, detailed project estimates, corporate workshops, account-specific proposals |

## Awareness-stage content: the lead magnet

Ben Hunt's ladder of awareness is the module's diagnostic for which headline actually lands: someone with "no problem, or it's not obvious" needs a wake-up jolt, not a comparison chart; someone who's "problem aware but has no solution" is asking Why? How? What?; someone comparing options is afraid of picking wrong; someone at the vendor-selection stage wants proof, guarantees and references. Diagnose the rung before you write the headline.

Five reliable awareness-stage formats: thought leadership and POV pieces, benchmark/maturity self-assessments ("where do we stand vs. the market"), guides/frameworks/checklists that teach without pitching, case-study digests and curated research roundups, and simple ROI/time-savings calculators. A lead magnet should ask nothing of the reader in return — no gate, no form. Save the form for later, when you genuinely need contact details: an interactive tool, a webinar seat, a demo request.

For distribution without a content studio: **NotebookLM** (Google) turns a research doc or deck into an auto-generated explainer podcast; **HeyGen** renders your own on-camera presence into an avatar-led video without a studio or camera crew, useful for scaling one script across personas or languages.

## Nurture: content chains by persona

The forever-nurture formula from the source material is "1/10/4": one message, roughly every 10 days, carrying content that's easy to consume in one sitting — a tip, a tool, a useful resource, a quote or industry note. A worked example in the source deck built a 7-email sales-rep sequence and a separate 16-email marketing sequence for a single persona, each email keyed to one specific pain or objection with its own call to action. Educational webinars and business breakfasts built around one persona's real question do double duty here — they work as both awareness content and a soft, low-commitment touchpoint inside a longer chain.

One structural distinction worth keeping: a genuine marketing broadcast (HTML template, unsubscribe link, sent via a marketing-automation platform, only to people who opted in) reads very differently from an individual email from a named rep (plain text, sent from a real inbox, to a business domain only) — and the two should usually be sequenced together, not as substitutes for each other.

## The intermediate offer (tripwire)

A tripwire — or "high-value offer" — sits between content and a full sales cycle. As the source material frames it: "demonstrate our expertise and prove the value; confirm the solution works and how it moves this person's KPIs; free, low-cost, or credited toward the project if they buy." It requires the account to act and hand over information, which is exactly what makes it a stronger signal than another content download.

Six formats, each illustrated in the source deck with a real published example: a targeted live demo on the account's own data (Softline-style); a business-process or maturity audit (Dassault Systèmes-style); a roadmap or target-architecture design session (Cisco- and Microsoft-style); a reference visit to a live customer site or innovation center; a hackathon or innovation day that works the account's actual problem live, in a few hours; and a paid technical consultation, free or for a small fixed fee (BASF-style). A worked example from the source deck: a 3-hour, coffee-break-included hackathon on "AI in acquiring and growing key accounts," priced modestly and positioned purely as a taste of the fuller engagement to come.

## Consideration & decision: buyer enablement

Late-funnel content has one job: arm your internal champion to make the case in a room you're not in, and make the ROI numerically undeniable.

- **Argument kits** for the champion: ROI benchmarks, competitor comparisons, calculators, best-practice case studies, demo recordings, testimonials, and a ready-made slide or email template.
- **Detailed case studies**, structured consistently: customer background, the problem, how they chose a vendor, why they chose you, the project in detail (phases, process, diagrams), the result in photos and numbers, a client quote, and a call to action.

Ten proposal mistakes worth checking your own template against: pitching the technical buyer's language to the economic buyer (and vice versa); no framing of what the account is losing by not buying; sending a bare spreadsheet with no narrative around it; no quantified financial benefit; no clear statement of what resources the client's own team needs to contribute; testimonials substituted for real case studies; no reference list of past clients; a generic, non-customized document; a confusing structure (the Minto Pyramid principle helps here); and no way to track whether the document was even opened.

## Agentic ABM in practice

#### MegaBrain: an agent workflow for this module

Point MegaBrain's desktop app at the GitHub repo holding your persona briefs, then prompt: *"Draft a 3-email nurture sequence for the CFO persona at [Named Account], referencing their Q2 FY26 earnings call and cost-reduction priorities."* Working directly on the local files, the agent returns a ready-to-edit 3-email sequence, tone-matched to the persona brief and flagged for a human pass before it goes out — no separate copywriting tool, no per-model contract to negotiate.

#### Other agent tools for content personalization

**Copy.ai** — rebranded from a copywriting tool into a full "GTM AI platform" in 2025-26; drafts persona- and account-specific email and landing copy at scale. **Mutiny** — pivoted in 2024-25 from website A/B-test personalization into an agent-first platform that generates personalized content across the whole deal cycle. **NotebookLM** (Google) — turns research and decks into an auto-generated explainer podcast for passive distribution. **HeyGen** — avatar-led video content, so one script scales across personas and languages without a camera crew.

## Exercises

#### Homework 1 — build your 3 core personas (Variant 2 format)

Pick your 3 highest-priority personas from the ICP and account plans you built in Modules 2-3. Gather source material for each: 5+ real customer or lost-deal interviews if you can get them, CRM win/loss notes, sales call recordings, and job postings for the role. For each persona, write down at least 3 pains, 3 objections, and 3 must-know points, plus what they compare you to. Cross-check every draft with your sales team before finalizing — they hear these objections live and will correct you. Save the result in the persona-brief-template.xlsx workbook and bring all 3 personas to Module 6.

#### Homework 2 — design one awareness-stage lead magnet

Choose one format: thought leadership, benchmark report, guide/checklist, case-study digest, or calculator. Define which persona it's for, the single pain it addresses, what the reader learns, and the one CTA at the end — no gate, no form. Draft a full section-by-section outline, and write the first section yourself, in full. Decide your distribution channel: an on-site article, a LinkedIn post, or an auto-generated podcast/video version.

#### Homework 3 — build a 9-email nurture chain for one persona

Pick one of your 3 personas and list every pain or objection you want the chain to address. Apply the 1/10/4 rule: one email roughly every 10 days, each carrying one easy-to-consume piece of content. Write a subject line and a one-CTA content plan for all 9 emails in sequence, moving from awareness toward decision. Produce two versions of the plan: a marketing-automation version (HTML template, unsubscribe link) and a from-a-rep version (plain text, sent from a real inbox).

#### Homework 4 — design your tripwire (intermediate) offer

Pick one format that fits your business: audit, demo, roadmap session, hackathon, or technical consult. Define the target audience, the pains it resolves, and the 3 concrete outcomes the account walks away with. Set the mechanics: duration, what you need from the client in return, and price (free, a small fixed fee, or credited to the main project if they buy). Write the one-page pitch you'd actually send to invite an account in.

#### Homework 5 — draft persona messaging with MegaBrain (required)

Confirm the MegaBrain desktop app you set up in Module 1's Homework 4 is still connected to your GitHub — this exercise uses it directly. Open the repo or folder with your persona briefs in MegaBrain and use the RISE prompt from this module to draft a full persona brief for one of your Homework 1 personas. Then prompt the agent: "Draft a 3-email nurture sequence for the {persona} at {a real target account from your list}, referencing their most recent public earnings call, product launch, or leadership change, and their likely cost/growth priorities." Read the output against your own persona brief and account intelligence, mark anything you'd cut, and rewrite the parts a human reader would flag as generic. Bring the edited sequence to Module 6.
